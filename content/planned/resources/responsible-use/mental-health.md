# Using AI for Mental Health Support

AI can be a low-pressure first step when you are considering support for your mental health. Writing out your feelings before talking to someone can help you clarify what you are experiencing. It is a useful starting point, but it has real limits.

## If You Are in Crisis

Callout: Colorado State Gold (#C8C372) tinted to 25% (#F1F0DC), heavy green border.

AI is not a substitute for emergency support. Speak to a counselor or a trusted person, or call a crisis line. Human support is irreplaceable when it matters most.

## What to Watch Out For

Six cards in a three-column grid (two on tablet, one on mobile). Each has a Font Awesome icon in a gold circle, an H3 and one or two sentences.

| Icon | Heading | Text |
|---|---|---|
| bullhorn | Sounds Sure When It Is Not | AI responds in a confident tone even when it is uncertain. Treat responses as a starting point, not the final word. |
| comments | Tells You What You Want to Hear | AI is designed to be agreeable and validating. That can feel supportive, but real growth sometimes needs honest challenge. Seek that from people you trust. |
| users | Not a Replacement for People | Over-reliance on AI can interfere with psychosocial development. The skills we build through human connection are not replaceable by AI interaction. |
| magnet | Built to Keep You Talking | Some chatbots use engagement techniques to keep you in conversation, including language that triggers guilt or fear of missing out. Notice if this is happening. |
| balance-scale | Shares Internet Biases | Training data often underrepresents certain groups and can draw on unvetted sources. |
| eye | Cannot See the Full Picture | AI has significant limits in psychological assessment. It cannot notice your body language, hear your tone, or respond to the full picture of who you are. |

## How to Use AI Well

- Use AI as a starting point, not a final answer. Verify information with qualified professionals or trusted sources.
- Be specific and break down complex requests. The more clearly you describe your situation, the more useful the response.
- Stay self-reflective. Notice when AI is helping you think things through and when it is telling you only what you want to hear.
- Remember that AI cannot confirm what is true in your specific life or circumstances.
- Protect your privacy. Check what a tool does with your conversations before sharing personal details.
