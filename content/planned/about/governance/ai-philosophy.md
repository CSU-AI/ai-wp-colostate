### The draft statement

At CSU, we recognize artificial intelligence as an increasingly influential part of higher education, professional life, and society. We expect faculty and instructors to engage with AI thoughtfully, critically, and in ways that reflect the needs of their disciplines, courses, and students.

Where AI is used, it should augment rather than replace human judgment, learner agency, relational teaching, and the sense of belonging that grows through meaningful interaction. CSU students should develop the critical literacy, data literacy, ethical discernment, adaptability, and professional readiness needed to navigate an AI-shaped world with agency and confidence. At the same time, instructional uses of AI should protect student privacy and dignity and preserve opportunities for productive struggle and reflection. CSU will support faculty through professional learning, consultation, shared resources, and ongoing opportunities for collective inquiry as tools, practices, and evidence continue to evolve.

Because AI technologies are changing rapidly and are often surrounded by exaggerated claims, our engagement should be grounded in evidence, context, and careful attention to their actual effects on learning, equity, sustainability, and the human experience of education. This does not presume universal AI adoption. Faculty and instructors are expected to use their disciplinary expertise, discretion, and academic freedom to assess relevancy and currency of needs within their field. Faculty and instructors have the expertise and obligation to determine where AI may strengthen teaching and learning outcomes, where its use may be inappropriate, and where intentional resistance may be both responsible and educationally valuable.
